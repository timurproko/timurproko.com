Chapter 01

History

VR is older than most people think — the first immersive machine predates the personal computer by two decades.

64

years of development, from Sensorama in 1956 to Meta Quest 2 in 2020.

  • 1956 — the first VR machine, a booth for all the senses
  • 1968 — the first head-mounted display
  • 2014 — Facebook buys Oculus and VR gains momentum
  • 2020 — standalone VR goes mainstream with Quest 2

Milestones

  1. 1956
    SensoramaCinematographer Morton Heilig's booth for up to four people combined full-colour 3D video, audio, vibration, smell and wind. Patented in 1962.
  2. 1968
    The Sword of DamoclesIvan Sutherland and Bob Sproull's first VR head-mounted display. Wire-frame shapes changed perspective with head tracking — but it hung from the ceiling and never left the lab.
  3. 1985
    VPL ResearchJaron Lanier and Thomas Zimmerman founded the first company to sell VR goggles and gloves — DataGlove, EyePhone HMD, AudioSphere. Its patents went to Sun Microsystems in 1999.
  4. 1990
    NASA VIEWThe Virtual Interface Environment Workstation: a stereoscopic HMD showing a computer-generated world or a remote camera feed, with 3D audio by Crystal River Engineering (1989).
  5. 1995
    Nintendo Virtual BoyThe first portable console with 3D graphics — and a commercial failure: monochrome, few games, uncomfortable to use.
  6. 2012
    Oculus Rift KickstarterPalmer Luckey's campaign raised $2.4 million and restarted consumer VR.
  7. 2014
    Facebook buys Oculus for $2 billionThe defining moment in VR's history — after it, the industry gained momentum rapidly.

Chapter 02

Theory

What are AR, VR, MR and XR — and why does VR ask so much of hardware, software and people?

AR vs. VR

Virtual reality

  • Computer-generated virtual environment
  • No view of the real world
  • R = synthetic world

Augmented reality

  • Virtual world merged with the physical one
  • Real + virtual, a composite view
  • R = real world

The reality–virtuality continuum

AR and VR are two ends of one spectrum. Everything in between — including augmented virtuality — is mixed reality.

Terms

  • VRVirtual RealityA computer-generated virtual world.
  • ARAugmented RealityAn enhanced physical world.
  • MRMixed RealityThe spectrum that includes AR and VR.
  • XRExtended RealityX is a wildcard for A, V or M.

Passthrough

Where the worlds meet

A headset's passthrough view blends the room with virtual content — the moment VR starts sliding along the spectrum toward mixed reality.

VR has extreme requirements

If 3D is challenging, VR is even more so — every part of the pipeline is pushed at once.

  • /01Visual qualityStereoscopic view doubles render time. Get it wrong and people get motion sick.
  • /02Sound qualitySpatial sound is part of presence, not an extra.
  • /03Real-time calculationsMovement synchronization and content optimization — baked textures and lighting.
  • /04Intuitive interactionsSpatial design for controllers and hand tracking.

Main problems with VR

  • /01Field of viewStill feels like looking through a tube — it needs to grow.
  • /02ControllersHand tracking isn't ideal yet and struggles with complex interactions.
  • /03HealthHeadaches, motion sickness and effects on vision.
  • /04Visual qualityGetting better, but not for long sessions — 30 minutes at most.
  • /05Degrees of freedomMovement is constrained. The future is MR with a VR option.

Tracking

Degrees of freedom describe how much of your movement a headset follows. 3DoF tracks only rotation; 6DoF adds position, so you can walk around a room-scale play area.

Two types of tracking

Outside-in vs. inside-out

  • Outside-in — cameras or base stations are mounted around the room
  • Inside-out — cameras are built into the headset itself, so no setup is needed

Body trackers

  • $120Tundra TrackerA smaller, better SteamVR tracking puck.
  • $270HaritoraXWaist and leg motion tracking, compatible with SteamVR.
  • $79DecaMoveNavigate with your hips in any SteamVR headset.
  • $130Vive TrackerTrack movement and bring real objects into VR.

Other trackers

Vive Facial Tracker

A $129.99 add-on that captures lip and jaw movement, so an avatar's face follows yours in real time.

Hand tracking

Why even bother with hand tracking?

Every big interface shift removed a layer between people and the computer — the iPhone famously dropped the stylus. Hands are the most natural input we have; controllers are still more precise, and tracking still struggles with complex interactions.

  • Controllers: PlayStation VR2, Meta Quest 2, HTC Vive
  • Hands: built into Quest, or added with Ultraleap sensors

Controllers and Ultraleap sensors

  • UltraleapLeap Motion ControllerSmall, fast and accurate. For evaluation, or as a peripheral for headsets and screens.
  • UltraleapStereo IR 170Wider field of view and longer tracking range, for evaluation and integration.
  • UltraleapUltraleap 3DiA ruggedized camera for public interactive screens.

BMW's factory of the future

NVIDIA and BMW blend real and virtual worlds so every factory has a "digital twin". Each line can build up to 10 different cars, and global teams reconfigure it together in real time on Omniverse — from Revit, CATIA or point clouds, with every change visible live.
